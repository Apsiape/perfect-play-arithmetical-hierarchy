\caption{The running example: the tree of four-letter words, with density weight $\tfrac34\,16^{-m}$ on each word of length $m$ (Section~\ref{sec:tree}). (a)~Each bridge $\btrans_g$ carries the corner $e_{g-1}$ onto $e_g$ and doubles density weights (Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge}); the child $A_\alpha\xi=v^*a_\alpha\xi$ of a parent $\xi$ is the vector of $e_0$ that the four bridges carry onto $a_\alpha\xi$; since $a_\alpha$ keeps weights, the child has one sixteenth of the parent's weight (Section~\ref{sec:fourbridge}). (b)~Bar widths are proportional to the square roots of the weights, and the shaded bars are a parent and child as in~(a); with four bridges the four children exactly tile their parent, so every row has the same width and the sum of all square roots diverges, although the weights sum to one. (c)~A fifth bridge gives the ratio $1/32$, each row covers $1/\sqrt2$ of the row above, and the sum is finite: this is the zero-error threshold of Section~\ref{sec:communication}.}\label{fig:running}
